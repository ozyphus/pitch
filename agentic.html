One agent mentors the learner in the page. Another builds, verifies, and ships the platform behind it. Same tools, same memory, same quality gates.

01 · The mentor designed

A guide, not a chatbot

Open chat causes cognitive offloading — you feel helped and retain nothing. The mentor runs a mastery loop instead: assess, scaffold, practice, check. It's grounded in the lesson text and your history, it withholds answers until you've built, and it plans routes you can read — every step of the trace visible.

hi-fi design — the planner trace, route ready
02 · The maker in build

Generation as watchable jobs

The feed tracks ~25k research items and knows exactly what the catalog is missing. Drafting agents turn the gaps into lessons — grounded in exemplars from shipped, verified work, never free-associating. Every draft lands in a review queue: draft → review → publish. Nothing auto-ships.

03 · The method live

This is how it's already built

  • 25 codified agent pipelines — lesson, paper, track, audio, video, deck. New content means invoking a skill, not starting over.
  • Agent fleets — rollouts and reviews fan out to parallel agents; one review pass ran 54 agents and fixed 46 findings before ship.
  • Autonomy — The Daily Delve researches, writes, narrates, and publishes itself every morning. Overnight runs ship lessons.
  • Verification — agents adversarially test depth, reproduce numbers against scipy, and screenshot every surface before commit.

Agents create, agents check — one person decides.
